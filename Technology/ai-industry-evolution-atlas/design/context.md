# Context

goal: Show that the current AI boom is the visible crest of a longer, cumulative history, including repeated breakthroughs, hype cycles, setbacks, winters, social concerns, and renewed acceleration.

user: C-level viewers are the primary scan audience; the experience must also remain understandable and useful to a global general audience.

JTBD: Understand the full evolution of AI in roughly 30 seconds, then explore any era and trace every substantive claim or plotted value to credible evidence.

framing: The evolution of AI from research field to global industry, rather than implying that a mature AI industry existed throughout the entire historical period.

platform: Public interactive web dashboard for the existing dashboard portfolio, deployed to a client-facing URL.

experience: Hybrid editorial atlas: a high-level explorable overview followed by an era-based narrative with progressive detail.

geography: Global. Surface the countries and regions that are historically relevant to each development without forcing a fixed US-centric structure.

timeline: Full historical arc through 2025, with 2026 explicitly marked as H1 or partial-year wherever applicable.

primary signal: Public interest in AI.

evidence model:
- Do not imply that one continuously comparable public-interest metric exists across the full timeline.
- Use the strongest available public-interest evidence for each era, with visible labels when the measurement method changes.
- Keep unlike measures visually and semantically distinct rather than merging them into a falsely precise composite index.
- Support the attention story with sourced milestone annotations spanning research, products, business, policy, public response, and social consequences.
- Distinguish direct measurements from proxies, estimates, and editorial classifications.
- Provide a methodology and bibliography; make each substantive detail traceable to a credible source.

executive story:
1. Breakthroughs compound across decades.
2. Hype repeatedly outruns technical or commercial readiness.
3. The latest public-attention cycle has accelerated beyond earlier cycles, subject to what the evidence can defensibly establish.

tone: Neutral, documentary, globally aware, and intellectually honest. Do not soften negative chapters or dramatize evidence beyond what sources support.

scope v1:
- One main historical atlas
- Era filters
- Overview suitable for a 30-second executive scan
- Scroll-led era narrative
- Detail panel for sourced events
- Public-interest evidence charts
- Global milestones and relevant geographic context
- Companies, notable systems/models, investment, regulation, and social response when they materially explain an era
- AI winters, failed promises, job anxiety, bias, surveillance, copyright conflict, safety concerns, and regulatory backlash
- Methodology, source notes, and bibliography
- Responsive behavior and accessible interaction appropriate to a public portfolio project

non-goals:
- Predicting the future of AI
- Presenting 2026 partial-year values as comparable with complete annual data
- Claiming a single objective measure of the AI industry's overall health
- Exhaustively cataloguing every paper, company, model, product, or policy
- Ranking countries or companies without a defensible comparable dataset
- Closely reproducing the supplied editorial reference

visual originality requirements:
- Preserve the user's desired core visual idea from the reference: a branching evolutionary map made from flower-like radial charts, with a magnified selected bloom.
- Treat the botanical structure as an explicit requirement, not merely optional inspiration.
- Transform the industry framing, page composition, spatial system, chart grammar, palette, typography, wording, and interaction model.
- Use AI paradigms, eras, public-attention evidence, milestones, setbacks, and consequences to determine the branches and radial encodings.
- The result must read as an original interactive documentary dashboard, not a digital recreation of the reference's magazine spread.

success:
- A C-level viewer can grasp the complete arc and three-part executive story in 30 seconds.
- A general viewer can understand how to read the dashboard without prior knowledge of AI history.
- A viewer can explore an era and trace every substantive claim to a credible source.
- Partial-year, proxy, estimated, and editorially classified data are unmistakably labeled.
- The final dashboard is original, responsive, accessible, publicly deployed, and documented in the project and portfolio READMEs.

open assumptions:
- The portfolio's existing static web approach can support the intended interactions and GitHub Pages deployment.
- Credible public-interest evidence can be assembled for each era, but the measures will not be directly comparable across the full arc.
- Executive scanning and general-audience learning can coexist through progressive disclosure.

risks:
- Historical public interest is difficult to quantify consistently before modern search and survey data.
- A visually continuous timeline could accidentally imply comparability between unlike measures.
- Too many milestones could overwhelm the executive-level story.
- Global coverage may still reflect archival and language bias in available English-language sources.
- Current and 2026 claims may change quickly and require a visible data cutoff date.

research gate: Do not begin UI implementation until the source architecture and at least two materially distinct visual directions have been reviewed.
